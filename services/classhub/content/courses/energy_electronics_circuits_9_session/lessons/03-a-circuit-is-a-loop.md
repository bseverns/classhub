**Mission:** Build your first simple circuit and prove that energy needs a complete loop.

## Build
- Start with a low-voltage power source, two wires, and one output.
- Build a complete loop that powers the output.
- Open the loop on purpose, then close it again and notice what changes.

## Reflection
- Explain the difference between an open circuit and a closed circuit.
- Take a photo that clearly shows the loop path.

## Teacher prep
- Build and test one sample circuit before class.
- Mark safe power sources and keep high-voltage devices out of reach.
- Decide whether students will use breadboards or snap-circuit style parts.

## Materials
- LEDs or buzzers
- Batteries or low-voltage power packs
- Breadboards or snap-circuit parts
- Jumper wires
- Chromebooks

## Agenda
- 0-10 min: Mini-lesson on loops and safe power.
- 10-20 min: Teacher demo of closed versus open circuit.
- 20-42 min: Student build time.
- 42-50 min: Intentionally open and close the loop.
- 50-60 min: Photo and explanation.

## Checkpoints
- Circuit works in closed-loop form.
- Student can make it stop by opening the loop.
- Student explains why the output changes.

## Common stuck points + fixes
- "I have wires everywhere."
  - Remove all but the minimum loop and rebuild step by step.
- "The LED still does not light."
  - Reverse the LED and test with the same power source.

## Extensions
- Label the positive and return path on a paper sketch.
- Test a second output and compare how it behaves.
